# Sterling Auto Insurance — Billing, Renewal & Reinstatement

## Paying your premium
Premiums can be paid in full or in monthly installments. You can enroll in **autopay** in the app to avoid missed payments. Your due date and balance are shown on your billing screen.

## Policy term and renewal
Auto policies run for a **12-month term** with an effective date and an expiration/renewal date shown on your declarations page. Before the renewal date, Sterling sends a renewal offer with the new term's premium. Renewing continues your coverage into the next term starting on the renewal date.

## What renewing does — and does not do
Renewal extends your coverage **forward** into a new term. It starts coverage on the renewal date going forward. **Renewing a policy does not backdate or extend coverage to a date before the new term begins.** A loss is only covered if a policy with the relevant coverage was **in force on the date of the loss**.

## Lapses and reinstatement
If a policy lapses (for example, for non-payment), there is a gap with **no coverage**. You may be able to **reinstate** the policy, sometimes with a lapse fee. Reinstatement restores coverage from the reinstatement date forward. Any loss that occurred **during** the lapse generally remains uncovered, because no coverage was in force at the time of that loss.

## Changing your renewal or coverage now to affect a past claim
Because coverage must be in force on the date of loss, changing, renewing, reinstating, or adding coverage **today** cannot make a **previously denied** claim become approved if the loss happened before that coverage was in force. If you believe coverage actually was in force at the time of your loss (for example, a payment was made on time, or the loss date is recorded incorrectly), that is handled as an **appeal/review** of the claim — a Sterling specialist can investigate. See the Denials & Appeals article.
